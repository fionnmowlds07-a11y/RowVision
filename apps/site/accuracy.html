It says when not to believe it.

A number with no warning on it looks the same whether the footage was good or hopeless. RowVision rates every result, and would rather show no score than one it cannot stand behind.

Every result has a rating.

The rating is the share of your angle readings expected to fall within 5° of the truth. It is marked as estimated, because it has not yet been checked against enough hand-marked footage to be called measured.

Excellent90% and upClose, side-on and steady.
Great80 to 89%Trust the scores.
Good70 to 79%Trust the pattern more than any one degree.
Fair60 to 69%A guide. Film closer or more side-on next time.
PoorUnder 60%The overall score stays hidden until you have read the warning.

What moves the rating: how big you are in the picture, how far the camera is from side-on, and how much your own strokes disagree with each other.

Where the camera is matters most.

Rowing happens along the boat, so a camera looking along the boat sees almost none of it. Move the launch round the crew and see what RowVision does with footage from there.

What it tells you.

Messages the app gives when the footage cannot carry a result.

  • The rowers are too small in this video to measure accurately, so no scores were given.The boat was too far off. Film closer, so the boat fills most of the frame width.
  • Too much of this video was filmed from in front of or behind the boat, so the results are unreliable and no scores were given.Your numbers are still shown. The scores are not.
  • The camera moved to the other side of the boat during this video, so only the part with the best view was measured.From the other side the crew faces the other way, so the two parts cannot be measured as one.
  • Another boat crossed yours for part of the time it was in view. Those frames were left out.So a rower in the other crew is never read as you.
  • Your body angle at the catch can’t be read from this camera angle on your side of the boat.Sweep rowers lean out towards their rigger, which hides the forward lean from some angles.

What it will not claim.

  • Knee angles are shown and never scored. They are the least certain thing it reads.
  • The targets come from published studies, mostly on rowing machines, and have not yet been reviewed by a coach.
  • Blade work is not measured. RowVision reads the body, not the oar.
  • It gives coaching feedback, not medical advice.

What you can pull out of it.

These are cut from the sample sheet. Each one answers something a coach asks from the launch.

Who is short, and who is over-reaching?

Each dot is a rower’s typical stroke and the line through it is how much it varies. The shaded band is the target and a hollow dot is outside it. Here seat 2 reaches only 21.7° while bow lays back to 28.5°.

Where does the crew come apart?

Everyone’s stroke, laid over each other from catch to next catch. Seat 3’s line falls away first on the drive, and bow’s finish comes after everyone else’s.

Who opens the back too soon?

When the legs (green), back (purple) and arms (orange) move in the boat, on one clock. Seat 3 is the one with purple ahead of green, and the sheet says so in a sentence at the top of the page.

Is it every stroke, or just some?

One dot for each stroke, showing how much of the drive has gone when the back starts to open. Beside it is the order each rower comes forward: arms, back, then legs.

Who is early, and who is late?

Every catch and finish against the rower the boat is timed from, and how steady that gap is. Seat 2 catches 60 ms late here. Timing is good to about 50 ms, so it is shown and never scored.

Does it hold when they tire?

The first half of the clip against the second, rower by rower. The biggest change is picked out: bow’s layback grew by 4.3°.

What should each rower work on?

Scores at the catch, the drive, the finish and for rhythm. The pale cell in a row is where that rower loses most: the drive for seat 3, the catch for seat 2, the finish for bow.

Every number, for every rower.

The sheet ends its detail with one table. Anything outside a scored target is in bold, and anything that could not be seen reliably is a dash.

Catch
Body angle, knee angle, shin from vertical and elbow angle.
Drive
How far the legs lead the back, when the arms start to bend, how far the body swings, and stroke length in lengths of the rower’s own trunk.
Finish
Layback, knee angle and elbow angle.
Rhythm
Stroke rate and how steady it is, drive and recovery in seconds, recovery against drive, and the drive’s share of the stroke.
In the crew
Catch and finish against the rower you are timed from, and how much each gap varies.
Hand-over
When the back opens, how far the legs are down by then, when the legs are done, and the order on the recovery.
With every result
The targets used, the strokes measured, and an accuracy rating for each rower.

What it will not claim.

  • The hand-over is worked out from body angles, not force. It shows when each part moves, not how hard.
  • Knees and shins are a rough guide and never scored.
  • A short clip has no first half and second half, and the sheet says so.
  • PDFs are made on your computer and saved where you choose. The photo report shows people’s faces, so ask your crew before you pass it on.

How it works

Film. Pick your seat. Read.

01

Film from the launch

Side-on is best. Hold the phone level and keep the crew in frame. Ordinary phone video is enough.

02

Say where you sit

Choose boat, side and seat. RowVision finds every rower and locks on to you.

03

Your joints, tracked

A pose model marks 17 points on your body in every frame. The bright side is nearest the camera.

04

Angles and scores

Knee, hip, back and arm angles at each catch and finish, scored against targets you can adjust. It tells you when the footage isn't good enough to trust.

Why analyse your stroke

Feel lies. Angles don't.

You can't see yourself row. Video shows you, but by eye it's hard to judge whether your back opened before your legs finished, or whether your catch changed when you got tired.

The sequence

Legs, then back, then arms.

The drive hands power from legs to back to arms, in that order. Open the back too early and the legs push against nothing. These are the real curves from the sculling clip on the home page, stroke after stroke.

Consistency

Stroke 1 should look like stroke 30.

Crews win by moving the same way every stroke. RowVision lays your strokes over each other, so drift at the catch shows up straight away.

About

Hi, I'm Fionn.

I'm a rower and a first-year Immersive Software Engineering student at the University of Limerick, based in Cork.

I built RowVision because I wanted to see my own stroke. Coaches in the launch have eight people to watch and a few seconds each. I wanted the numbers: when my legs finished, when my back opened, whether my catch held up on the last piece.

So I wrote software that does it from the phone footage crews already take.

How it finds your crew.

It follows people, not the boat
Every person in the picture is tracked through the clip. Those who move with a stroke rhythm are rowers. A cox, a coach in the launch and people on the bank are left out.
A crew sits on one line
Rowers in one boat fall along a straight line from any camera position. That is how two crews racing side by side are kept apart.
Seats are numbered from the bow
Rowers face the stern, so the way the crew faces gives the order. You tap yourself once and say your seat, and the rest follow from that.
More than one boat
If the clip shows several crews, RowVision stops and asks you to tap yours. When another boat crosses yours, those frames are left out.
One rower or the whole crew
Measure only yourself, or every seat at once. A whole-crew result shows each rower beside the others, and prints as one sheet or a sheet for each side. See the reports.

Sweep boats are read by side.

A sweep rower leans out towards their rigger, so the two sides of a boat look different to any camera that is not exactly side-on. RowVision knows which side each seat rows on and corrects each one for it.

This was checked on an eight filmed by a drone from every angle. About 300 catches were compared with the same rower’s side-on reading.

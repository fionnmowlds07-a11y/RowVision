You can’t see yourself row.

A coach in the launch sees a crew for a few strokes at a time, from one side, while steering. RowVision is made by a rower who wanted to see every stroke of an outing afterwards, with numbers on it.

What it measures.

Knee, hip and elbow angles and how far the body swings, through every stroke. It finds each catch and each finish and scores you there.

Knees and elbows are read from the side of you nearest the camera, because the far side is often hidden by your own body and the boat.

Where the targets come from.

The starting targets are our reading of published rowing studies, most of them done on rowing machines. They have not yet been reviewed by a coach, and the app says so.

They are a place to start. You can move any range, and choose which measures count towards your score.

What it will not do.

  • It will not give a number it cannot stand behind. Poor footage gets a warning, and footage that is too distant gets no score.
  • It will not send your video anywhere. The analysis runs on your own computer.
  • It is coaching feedback, not medical advice.

The mark.

The logo is the product in one drawing: a rower at the finish, drawn as a single line, with the shoulder, elbow, hip and knee picked out as points. Those are the joints RowVision reads.

Where it is now.

RowVision is in a private preview with a small group of adult rowers. The desktop app for Windows and Linux comes first. See what works today and what comes next.

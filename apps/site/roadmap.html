Where it is now.

RowVision is in a private preview with a small group of adult rowers. This page says plainly what works, what is being built and what is only planned.

Working today In the app

  • Joints tracked through a clip, for any boat from a single to an eight.
  • Every catch and finish found, with body angle, knees, arms, rhythm and rate at each.
  • Scores against targets you can change, and what to work on next.
  • An accuracy rating on every result, and no score when the footage cannot carry one.
  • Frame-by-frame stepping with your joints and angles over the picture.
  • Every score traced to the frames it came from.
  • A whole crew measured at once, with timing against the rower each seat follows.
  • Printed photos of every catch and finish, in four layouts. See the reports.
  • A stroke-by-stroke chart, and whether you fade in the second half.
  • Saved clips measured again in seconds when the measuring improves.

Being worked on Next

  • Version 0.3.0: the printed statistics sheet, the power hand-over, and any two rowers compared, from one clip or two.
  • Installers for Windows and Linux, with updates.
  • Testing on ordinary Windows laptops, and using the graphics card where there is one.
  • Footage filmed from ahead of the boat. The correction for camera angle has so far been checked from the side and the stern quarter.
  • Measuring both parts of a clip when the camera crosses to the other side of the boat.

Planned for later Later

  • An Android app.
  • Comparing two clips side by side.
  • Coach accounts, and a coach’s review of the starting targets.
  • Under-18s, once parental consent and safeguarding are in place.

Not planned Set aside

  • Blade height and blade work. A phone on a launch cannot measure it well enough to stand behind.

How it gets better.

Each change to the measuring is checked on real clips before it is kept, and results from an older version are labelled, never quietly changed. One recent check used a drone clip of an eight filmed from every angle, and about 300 catches, to set how far a sweep rower leans out towards the rigger.

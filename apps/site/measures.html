Loading the strokeAngles are read from the joints as drawn, on the side nearest the camera.

Along the bottom is the knee angle for the whole clip. Round marks are catches and square marks are finishes, as in the app.

At the catch.

Body angle at the catch Scored
How far your trunk leans forward from upright when the blade goes in. The starting target is 26 to 31°.
Knee angle at the catch Shown
How far your legs are compressed. Shown as a rough guide and never scored, because knee angle depends on your proportions and is the hardest joint to read from a moving launch. From version 0.3.0 the lean of your shin is shown beside it.
Arms at the catch Shown
Whether your arms are long when the blade goes in.

Through the drive.

Legs before body Scored
Whether the legs start the drive before the back opens. This follows the coaching principle of legs, then body, then arms.
When the arms start to bend Shown
How far through the drive your elbows break. They should come in last.
Body swing Shown
The whole arc of your trunk, from forward at the catch to laid back at the finish.
Stroke length Shown
How far your hands travel along the boat, measured in lengths of your own trunk so it does not depend on how close the camera was.

At the finish.

Layback at the finish Scored
How far your trunk leans back when the blade comes out. The starting target is 20 to 25°.
Knee angle at the finish Shown
Whether the legs are flat. Straight legs are normal on the water and are never marked down.
Elbow angle at the finish Shown
How far the arms have drawn in.

Rhythm and timing.

Stroke rate
Strokes a minute, from the spacing of your catches, and how steady it stays.
Drive and recovery rhythm Scored
The share of each stroke spent on the drive. The target moves with your rate, because the recovery shortens as the rate comes up.
Recovery against drive Shown
How many times longer the recovery is than the drive, with the time of each in seconds.
Timing against the rower you follow
In a crew, how many thousandths of a second your catch and finish come before or after theirs. See who you follow.
Second half against first
With six strokes or more, whether each measure holds or fades as the clip goes on.

How the drive is handed over.

When the back opens Shown
How much of the drive has gone when your back starts to swing, stroke by stroke, and how far your legs are down by then.
Legs, back and arms in the boat Shown
When each part of each rower moves, on one clock from stroke’s catch.
Coming forward Shown
Whether the arms, then the back, then the legs lead the recovery.

This is worked out from body angles, not force, and comes with version 0.3.0. See it on a printed sheet.

How the score is made.

A measure scores 100 inside its target range and falls away in a straight line outside it. Your overall score needs both the catch and the finish to be scored, so an easy measure can never carry the result alone.

The starting targets are RowVision’s reading of published rowing studies, most of them done on rowing machines, and no range is wider than 5°. They have not yet been reviewed by a coach. You can move any range in Settings, and choose which measures count.

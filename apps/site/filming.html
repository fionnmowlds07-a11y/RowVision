Film it so it measures.

Any phone will do. Five habits make the difference between a rating of Poor and one you can train from.

  1. Stay beside the boat

    Side-on is where rowing shows. Up to about 25° off is fine. Past 60° the boat is end-on, and those strokes are not counted. Try the camera dial.

  2. Get close

    The rower you care about should be easy to pick out. If the crew is tiny in the picture, RowVision gives no score and asks you to film closer, so the boat fills most of the frame width.

  3. Stay on one side

    If the launch goes round the bow or stern, the crew turns round in the picture, and only the part with the better view is measured.

  4. Hold it for the strokes

    Three full strokes are needed for any score, and ten or more give a result worth trusting. Six lets RowVision say whether you fade in the second half.

  5. Keep the original file

    Use the video as the phone saved it. Copies sent through a messaging app are shrunk, and small rowers measure worse.

Things that do not matter.

  • A moving launch. Angles are read between your own joints, so the camera can drift, pan and zoom.
  • The weather. Bright days and flat grey ones both work.
  • A long video. Pick the 60 seconds you want looked at.
  • Other people in the shot. A cox, a coach and people on the bank are left out.

Things that do.

  • Another crew crossing in front of yours. Those frames are left out.
  • Filming from the bow or stern for most of the clip. You will get numbers and no scores.
  • Spray, glare or a blade hiding your body at the catch, stroke after stroke.

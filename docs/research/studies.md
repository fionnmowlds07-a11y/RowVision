# Studies

The evidence behind RowVision's technique targets and accuracy rating. See [the evidence summary](README.md) for how these studies are used and what they cannot show.

Citation details come from PubMed records, retrieved 5 November 2026. Reviewed studies were read at abstract level or better. The notes are summaries, so check the original paper before quoting a figure.

## Reviewed studies

### Trunk angle and spine

- Cardoso R, Rios M, Fonseca P et al. (2024). Assessment of Angular and Straight Linear Rowing Ergometers at Different Intensities of Exercise. *Sensors (Basel)*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39275598/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24175686) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11397995/)
  - 16 skilled rowers with international experience. Markerless full-body capture. Thorax about 26–32° forward at the catch and 18–28° back at the finish, below extreme intensity. Primary source for trunk targets.
- Penichet-Tomas A, Calavia-Carbajal S, Pueo B et al. (2025). Kinematic Analysis of Olympic and Traditional Rowing Mechanics at different Stroke Rates. *Int J Exerc Sci*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40708773/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.70252/RFXJ1471) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12289236/)
  - 13 elite national-level women at 18, 24 and 30 spm. Olympic-style trunk −33 to −36° at catch, knee 56–61° at catch and 167–169° at finish. Finish trunk of 44–49° is far above other studies, and the paper says its video tool is not yet validated.
- Bell G, Bennett J, Reynolds W et al. (2013). A Physiological and Kinematic Comparison of two Different Lean Back Positions During Stationary Rowing on a Concept II Machine. *J Hum Kinet*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24146710/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2013-0030) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3796848/)
  - 14 rowers. Knee about 44–52° at catch and mostly 160–164° at finish. Hip about 31–33° at catch, and about 117° (upright) or 145° (lean back) at finish. A greater lean back increased range of motion and handle travel.
- Li Y, Koldenhoven RM, Jiwan NC et al. (2023). Trunk and shoulder kinematics of rowing displayed by Olympic athletes. *Sports Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32677503/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2020.1781238)
  - 58 Olympic athletes at 18, 26 and 32 spm. Trunk and shoulder range of motion rose with stroke rate, and women showed more extended finish positions.
- Li Y, Koldenhoven RM, Jiwan NC et al. (2024). Intra-trunk and arm coordination displayed by Olympic rowing athletes. *Sports Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33666144/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2021.1883728)
  - 59 Olympic athletes. Trunk segments and upper arm moved mostly in phase, more so during the drive at higher rates.
- McGregor A, Anderton L, Gedroyc W (2002). The assessment of intersegmental motion and pelvic tilt in elite oarsmen. *Med Sci Sports Exerc*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12131255/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005768-200207000-00015)
  - 20 elite oarsmen scanned by MRI at four stroke positions. Rowers with no back pain history showed the greatest lower lumbar mobility at the catch.
- Strahan AD, Burnett AF, Caneiro JP et al. (2011). Differences in spinopelvic kinematics in sweep and scull ergometer rowing. *Clin J Sport Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21562418/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/JSM.0b013e31821a6465)
  - 10 high-level rowers. Sweep showed more lateral bend and axial rotation at the catch than sculling. Both came close to end-range lower lumbar flexion at the catch.
- Pollock CL, Jenkyn TR, Jones IC et al. (2009). Electromyography and kinematics of the trunk during rowing in elite female rowers. *Med Sci Sports Exerc*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204587/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e31818c1300)
  - 9 elite women in a 2000 m simulation. Little spinal excursion during peak force, with most extension occurring at the pelvis.
- McGregor AH, Patankar ZS, Bull AM (2005). Spinal kinematics in elite oarswomen during a routine physiological "step test". *Med Sci Sports Exerc*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15947728/)
  - 12 elite women in a step test. Stroke length stayed fairly consistent as intensity rose, with kinematic changes at the catch and finish.
- Holt PJ, Bull AM, Cashman PM et al. (2003). Kinematics of spinal motion during prolonged rowing. *Int J Sports Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14598197/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2003-43273)
  - 13 elite oarsmen over a one-hour piece. Quantified how lumbar and pelvic motion relate to force during prolonged rowing.
- Mackenzie HA, Bull AM, McGregor AH (2008). Changes in rowing technique over a routine one hour low intensity high volume training session. *J Sports Sci Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24149955/) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761929/)
  - 6 elite sweep oarsmen. Technique and force were largely maintained over a one-hour session.

### Legs, hips and body position

- Engstrom I, Anderson K, Bez E et al. (2023). The effect of foot-stretcher position and stroke rate on ergometer rowing kinematics. *PLoS One*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37167317/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0285676) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10174490/)
  - 11 college rowers at 22, 26 and 32 spm. Stroke rate changed kinematics at all joints. Foot-stretcher changes mainly affected the ankle and hip. Primary source for drive timing.
- Buckeridge EM, Weinert-Aplin RA, Bull AM et al. (2016). Influence of foot-stretcher height on rowing technique and performance. *Sports Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27256844/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2016.1185459)
  - 10 men at four foot-stretcher heights. Raising the stretcher reduced horizontal foot force, stroke length and pelvis range of motion.
- Buckeridge EM, Bull AM, McGregor AH (2015). Biomechanical determinants of elite rowing technique and performance. *Scand J Med Sci Sports*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25039605/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.12264)
  - 20 elite women. Hip kinematics predicted foot force, and knee and lumbar-pelvic kinematics influenced its horizontal component.
- Fumoto M, Sera Y, Azuma K et al. (2020). Body Motion and Rowing Performance: Association between Hip Angle and Rowing Performance: A Pilot Study. *Keio J Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31969524/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2302/kjm.2019-0007-OA)
  - 11 men, pilot study. Greater knee flexion at the catch accompanied more posterior pelvic rotation, and hip angle variance was linked to test time.

### Stroke timing and sequencing

- McGregor AH, Bull AM, Byng-Maddick R (2004). A comparison of rowing technique at different stroke rates: a description of sequencing, force production and kinematics. *Int J Sports Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15346237/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2004-820936)
  - 10 collegiate men at three rate bands. Force curves and lumbopelvic kinematics changed markedly with rate.
- Pollock CL, Jones IC, Jenkyn TR et al. (2012). Changes in kinematics and trunk electromyography during a 2000 m race simulation in elite female rowers. *Scand J Med Sci Sports*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21129036/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2010.01249.x)
  - 9 elite women in a 2000 m simulation. Later in the race, leg extension and arm flexion became more separated and trunk extension was delayed.
- Cerne T, Kamnik R, Vesnicer B et al. (2013). Differences between elite, junior and non-rowers in kinematic and kinetic parameters during ergometer rowing. *Hum Mov Sci*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23756001/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.humov.2012.11.006)
  - 5 elite, 5 junior and 5 non-rowers at 20, 26 and 34 spm. Elite technique stayed consistent across rates. Juniors followed similar principles but changed stroke length with rate.
- Ettema G, Haug A, Ludvigsen TP et al. (2025). The role of stroke rate and intensity on rowing technique. *Sports Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36300635/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2022.2135457)
  - 12 competitive men. Stroke rate was regulated mostly through recovery time, while drive time depended more on intensity.
- Duchene Y, Simon FR, Ertel GN et al. (2025). The stroke rate influences performance, technique and core stability during rowing ergometer. *Sports Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38205960/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2024.2301992)
  - 24 high-level rowers. Higher rates raised segment power early in the drive and delayed trunk extension.
- Simon FR, Ertel GN, Duchene Y et al. (2023). Prediction of rowing ergometer performance by technical and core stability parameters. *J Sports Sci*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37253176/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2023.2219076)
  - 24 high-level rowers. Trunk power was the best predictor of handle power, and greater trunk range of motion raised trunk power.
- Soper C, Hume PA (2004). Towards an ideal rowing technique for performance : the contributions from biomechanics. *Sports Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15462614/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2165/00007256-200434120-00003)
  - Review of the biomechanics of sculling and sweep. Concludes that research on an ideal technique is limited.

### On-water performance and coaching

- Legge N, Draper C, Slattery K et al. (2024). On-water Rowing Biomechanical Assessment: A Systematic Scoping Review. *Sports Med Open*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39331267/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-024-00760-2) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11436553/)
  - Systematic scoping review of on-water rowing biomechanics. 27 of 1,659 records met criteria. Maps which measures of time, space and force have been studied.
- Holt AC, Aughey RJ, Ball K et al. (2020). Technical Determinants of On-Water Rowing Performance. *Front Sports Act Living*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33345160/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2020.589013) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739831/)
  - 47 races across singles and pairs. Sculling catch angle about −62° and sweep about −48°. Links measures of technique to boat velocity.
- Legge N, Slattery K, O'Meara D et al. (2024). Physical and technical attributes associated with on-water rowing performance in junior and elite rowers. *J Sports Sci*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39351682/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2024.2408521)
  - 28 juniors and 24 elite rowers in single sculls. Elite rowers had greater gate force and distance per stroke. Catch angle was linked to anthropometry and strength.
- Legge N, Draper C, O'Meara D et al. (2026). Assessment of rowing biomechanics during single sculling using functional clustering. *J Sports Sci*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41631299/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2026.2623564)
  - 25 elite rowers. Functional clustering found distinct technique patterns, supporting the idea that good technique varies by individual.
- Chen S, Zhang Y, Li X et al. (2026). Decoupling of kinetic and kinematic asymmetry in elite sculling: catch slip synchronization is more closely associated with boat speed than force balance. *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42174642/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-01755-0) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13371240/)
  - 12 elite scullers. Catch timing between sides was more closely linked to boat speed than force balance.
- Baumann E, Schmid MJ (2024). Insights from expert coaches on technical performance evaluation in rowing: a pilot study. *Front Sports Act Living*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39450119/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2024.1448797) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11499098/)
  - Interviews with four national team coaches about how they judge technique on the water.
- Legge N, Slattery K, Watsford M et al. (2025). Movement competency in rowing: the key to an effective stroke. *Front Sports Act Living*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40612116/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2025.1601563) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12223773/)
  - Perspective article defining movement competency for rowers, including mobility and stability demands.

### Ergometer against on-water rowing

- Wilson F, Gissane C, Gormley J et al. (2013). Sagittal plane motion of the lumbar spine during ergometer and single scull rowing. *Sports Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23898686/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2012.726640)
  - 19 elite men. Lumbar flexion rose more during a step test on the ergometer (4.4°) than in a single scull (1.3°).
- Trompeter K, Weerts J, Fett D et al. (2021). Spinal and Pelvic Kinematics During Prolonged Rowing on an Ergometer vs. Indoor Tank Rowing. *J Strength Cond Res*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31373977/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000003187)
  - 8 scull rowers. Ergometer rowing gave more lumbar range of motion and less posterior pelvic tilt at the catch than tank rowing.
- Lamb DH (1989). A kinematic comparison of ergometer and on-water rowing. *Am J Sports Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2729487/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/036354658901700310)
  - 30 rowers filmed on water and on an ergometer. Arm segment kinematics differed significantly between the two.
- Steer RR, McGregor AH, Bull AM (2006). A comparison of kinematics and performance measures of two rowing ergometers. *J Sports Sci Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24198681/) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3818674/)
  - 12 rowers. Lumbopelvic kinematics differed between two ergometers, and repeatability was assessed over repeat sessions.

### Back health and injury

- Nugent FJ, Vinther A, McGregor A et al. (2021). The relationship between rowing-related low back pain and rowing biomechanics: a systematic review. *Br J Sports Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33397675/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102533)
  - Systematic review, 22 studies and 429 participants. Healthy rowers showed neutral or anterior pelvic rotation at the catch, greater hip range of motion and a flatter low back at the finish.
- Wilson F, Thornton JS, Wilkie K et al. (2021). 2021 consensus statement for preventing and managing low back pain in elite and subelite adult rowers. *Br J Sports Med*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33685861/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-103385)
  - Consensus statement on preventing and managing low back pain in adult rowers.
- Athy V, Hach S, Anderson H et al. (2023). Examining the Peer-Reviewed Published Literature Regarding Low Back Pain in Rowing: A Scoping Review. *Int J Sports Phys Ther*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36793564/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26603/001c.67836) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9897042/)
  - Scoping review. Prolonged ergometer use and previous back pain were principal risk factors.
- Buckeridge EM, Bull AM, McGregor AH (2016). Incremental training intensities increases loads on the lower back of elite female rowers. *J Sports Sci*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26090702/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2015.1056821)
  - 12 women. Lower back shear and compressive forces rose with stroke rate, along with lumbar-pelvic flexion.

### Junior and adolescent rowers

- Ng L, Campbell A, Burnett A et al. (2015). Spinal Kinematics of Adolescent Male Rowers with Back Pain in Comparison with Matched Controls During Ergometer Rowing. *J Appl Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26252195/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1123/jab.2015-0012)
  - 10 adolescent men with back pain against 10 without. Those with pain spent more of the drive near end-range upper lumbar flexion.
- Ng L, Campbell A, Burnett A et al. (2013). Gender differences in trunk and pelvic kinematics during prolonged ergometer rowing in adolescents. *J Appl Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22814388/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1123/jab.29.2.180)
  - 20 adolescents. Boys held more posterior pelvic tilt and thoracic flexion than girls, and both changed over time.
- Anpilogov IE, Kruchynsky NH, Postnikov EB (2026). Stability of Rowing Technique and Specificity of Training Load: A Pilot Longitudinal Study in Young Athletes. *Sports (Basel)*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42188590/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports14050214) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13210610/)
  - 7 juniors aged about 16.6, pilot study. Stroke phase durations shifted across training blocks while overall stroke duration stayed stable.

### Video and pose estimation accuracy

- Scataglini S, Abts E, Van Bocxlaer C et al. (2024). Accuracy, Validity, and Reliability of Markerless Camera-Based 3D Motion Capture Systems versus Marker-Based 3D Motion Capture Systems in Gait Analysis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. *Sensors (Basel)*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38894476/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24113686) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11175331/)
  - Systematic review and meta-analysis of 22 papers. Markerless hip and knee agreement ranged from moderate to excellent in gait.
- Wang H, Su B, Lu L et al. (2024). Markerless gait analysis through a single camera and computer vision. *J Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38430608/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2024.112027)
  - Single-camera gait study. Reports good agreement with marker-based capture and examines the effect of camera angle and distance.
- Uhlrich SD, Falisse A, Kidziński Ł et al. (2023). OpenCap: Human movement dynamics from smartphone videos. *PLoS Comput Biol*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37856442/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011462) · [Free full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10586693/)
  - OpenCap: validated movement analysis from two or more smartphones.
- Turner JA, Chaaban CR, Padua DA (2024). Validation of OpenCap: A low-cost markerless motion capture system for lower-extremity kinematics during return-to-sport tasks. *J Biomech*. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38905926/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2024.112200)
  - Validated OpenCap against marker-based capture across 437 trials of return-to-sport tasks.
- Wade L, Needham L, Evans M, et al. (2023). Examination of 2D frontal and sagittal markerless motion capture: Implications for markerless applications. *PLoS One* 18(11): e0293917. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0293917)
  - 15 adults walking, using OpenPose from one camera. The camera-side knee and hip were far more reliable than the hidden side, with error spread about 69% and 108% greater on the hidden side.

# RowVision

## Purpose

RowVision analyses video of rowing. An athlete uploads launch footage, says which boat, side and seat they are in, and sees how each limb moves through the stroke against published technique targets, with a plain statement of how far the result can be trusted.

## Users

College and junior rowers, and the coaches who work with them. They review footage after a session, often on a laptop or phone at the boathouse, and want to know what to change in their next outing. Many users are minors.

## What makes it different

- It works on ordinary phone footage from a moving launch, at any angle, and says when the footage is not good enough.
- Every score can be traced to the exact frames and the published studies behind it.
- It would rather show "not scored" or "film closer" than give a number it cannot defend.

## Core workflow

1. Choose a clip, boat class, rowing side, number of boats and seat.
2. Pick your boat if the video shows more than one.
3. Read the accuracy rating and scores.
4. Open a measure to check it against freeze frames, the target and the 3D model.
5. Step through the footage frame by frame.

## Product rules

- The accuracy rating is always visible and marked as estimated until calibrated.
- When the rating is Poor, the overall score stays hidden until the viewer accepts a warning.
- Output is coaching feedback, not medical advice.
- Real footage shows identifiable athletes, many of them juniors, and stays out of the repository.

## Terminology

Catch, drive, finish, recovery, stroke side (port), bow side (starboard), sculling, seat (1 is bow), layback, stroke rate in strokes a minute.

## Platform

Web. Used on laptops and phones.

## Accessibility

Keyboard use for all stepping and zoom. Readable colour contrast. Colour never carries meaning alone.

## Voice

Plain, direct and encouraging, in British English, using the words rowers use.
